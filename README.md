
More than a system of control, **cybernetics** is the network of interactions between humans, machines, and environment. Defined as the study of communication, regulation, and feedback processes in living beings, machines, and organizations, cybernetics starts from the idea that the biological, the technological, and the social share common patterns : circularity, information, and adaptation.

Linked in its origins to **military, industrial, and capitalist power**, cybernetics still sustains forms of surveillance and accumulation. The term was popularized by **Norbert Wiener** in *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* (1948), in the postwar context, when radars, guided missiles, and predictive systems revealed its tight relation with the military-industrial apparatus. In the following decades, its influence expanded into **biology**, **psychology**, and the **social sciences**, giving rise to **systems theory** and the first developments in **artificial intelligence**.

In **Pangea**, cybernetics is not conceived only as a theory of control but as a **territory in dispute**, a field of possibilities to **reappropriate** and **reconfigure relational networks**.  
We ask:  
- Which bodies, knowledges, and languages are excluded from digital systems?  
We hack the notion of *control* to rehearse forms of interaction based on **reciprocity and diversity**.  
We propose a **decolonial cybernetics** that does not govern from above, but **weaves bonds** among humans, machines, and territories as a **living, multiple, pluriversal weave**.

## Key Authors

- **Norbert Wiener**: pioneer of the technical-mathematical approach to control and communication.  
- **Stafford Beer**: applied cybernetics to organizational management and social systems.  
- **Humberto Maturana** and **Francisco Varela**: with the theory of *autopoiesis*, redefined cybernetics from biology as **self-organizing living systems**.  
- **Donna Haraway**: with her *Cyborg Manifesto* (1985), re-signified cybernetics from **critical feminism**, presenting the **cyborg** as a figure that challenges dichotomies such as *human/machine* and *nature/culture*, opening possibilities for thinking hybrid, non-normative bodies beyond Western universalist narratives.  
- **Achille Mbembe**: showed how **digital control** articulates with **necropolitics**.  
- **Aníbal Quijano** and **Walter Mignolo**: revealed how **algorithmic systems** reproduce **colonial hierarchies of knowledge and power**.
